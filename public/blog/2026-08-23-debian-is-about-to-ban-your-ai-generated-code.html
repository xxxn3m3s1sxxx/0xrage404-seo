Debian is about to ban your AI-generated code. And the vote closes in six days. If you contribute to open source, this affects you whether you use Debian or not.

The Debian project just tabled a general resolution with EIGHT competing proposals — everything from a total ban on LLM-assisted contributions to a proposal titled, and I quote, "climate destruction is a deal breaker." The flagship ban states plainly: we will not allow direct contributions written with the use or assistance of large language models. Seven counter-proposals fight over how big the provenance sticker should be. Voting ends August 28th.

Here is the argument nobody wants to have out loud. Debian's strongest point is not copyright. It is not the environment. It is buried in their community statement: contributors who lean on a model never learn packaging, so they can never replace the maintainer who burns out. That is deskilling — and it compounds. Every senior maintainer today got there by breaking things as a junior. Delete the junior struggle and ten years from now there is no senior pipeline at all.

TACTICAL DEBRIEF: A blanket ban treats the symptom. Banning AI does not eliminate AI — it just makes disclosure harder. Copy-paste needs no permission slip. The projects that survive this era will be the ones that build verification INTO the contribution pipeline: provenance tracking, review depth tied to risk, evidence that a human actually understood what shipped. Policy statements age like milk; pipelines do not.

Whatever Debian decides, the real vote already happened — in every repo where nobody checks anymore.

Do not subscribe. Go read the actual proposals instead.
